What does a retail seller keep?

A side study to the paper. A one-month at-the-money straddle sold on SPY, QQQ, IWM, GLD and USO every month from January 2016 to July 2026, priced on the real consolidated quotes. On SPY, QQQ and IWM a retail seller kept about 90 to 95 cents of each paper dollar; crash months and margin calls, not trading costs, were the danger.

Costs barely matter on liquid funds. Crashes and margin do.

Writing premium equal to 5% of the account each month, a retail seller kept 95 cents of each paper dollar on SPY, 94 on QQQ and 90 on IWM after spreads and commissions, and 58 on USO, whose options trade far wider. Gold lost money before any cost, so it had no paper dollar to keep.

Size changes the picture. At 20% of the account, margin calls came often (SPY in 24 of 127 months), each forcing the position to be bought back at a bad moment and reopened smaller, and forced liquidation became the largest leak on SPY and QQQ. On IWM the same rule cut losses that kept growing, so the seller kept 118 cents per paper dollar, more than the paper trade itself.

The real risk is the crash month. Held to expiry at the modest size, the COVID trade (24 February to 20 March 2020) cost SPY 26.3%, IWM 34.8% and USO 29.4% of the account. Over ten simulated years, the chance of equity falling below half its starting value runs from 1.9% (QQQ) to 34.8% (GLD).

The trade. One short at-the-money straddle a month per fund, entered at 2:30 p.m. New York (11:30 a.m. on half-days) on the first trading day after each monthly expiry, held to the next one (24 to 32 days) and settled on the fund's close. The first entry was on 19 January 2016, the last on 20 July 2026, expiring 21 August 2026.
Paper dollar. One dollar of the trade's return at the mid price, before any cost: the yardstick for what is kept.
Retail costs. 80% of the quoted half-spread on every trade, after Muravyev and Pearson (2020), and $0.65 a contract per leg.
Margin limits. At entry the position is cut, if need be, so the margin it needs beyond the premium taken in stays within 90% of the account.
Forced liquidation. On any day the account cannot meet the margin requirement, the whole position is bought back at that day's mark and reopened smaller at the same strike.
Leaks. Each friction's share is its Shapley value: its average effect over all 24 orders in which the four frictions can be switched on.

What free data gets wrong about the cost

Someone pricing these trades from a free feed would misjudge the spread cost, because on these funds the free feed's quotes run wider than the consolidated market's: from 3% wider on USO to 2.1 times as wide on SPY. Using the ratio measured between the two feeds from 17 September to 9 October 2026, and assuming it held for ten years, the free-data user overstates the spread leak by 2.7 cents per dollar on SPY, 1.0 on QQQ, 1.7 on IWM and 0.9 on USO. It is an error in the estimate only, never subtracted as cash.

No reliable break-even level, and costs are not what moves it

If costs rose fast enough with fear, there would be an index level above which selling stopped paying. Grouped by each fund's own volatility index, the monthly result net of the spread cost swings by several volatility points from one group to the next (SPY's middle three groups: +2.15, −1.05, +1.68), while the spread cost stays at or below 0.14 of a point on the equity funds and peaks at 1.2 on USO, the widest market here. A straight line fitted through the months crosses zero for every fund, but its 90% interval is 15 to 39 index points wide, and 8 to 28% of resamples cross outside the data altogether. There is no reliable break-even.

Volatility points. A month's profit at the mid price divided by the straddle's vega at entry, less the spread cost, so that every fund sits on one scale.
The line. Least squares through all of a fund's months, not through the five group means; months beyond the axis pull it too.

Ten simulated years at the modest size

Resampling the real monthly results into 10,000 ten-year paths shows what a decade of selling could feel like at 5% of the account, after spreads and commissions. The odds are conditional on the crashes that 2016 to 2026 happened to contain.

Predictions, written before any data was bought

What this cannot show

  • The cost fractions are borrowed. 80% of the quoted half-spread comes from Muravyev and Pearson's study of single-stock options (2020). A working paper on retail option trading (Bogousslavsky and Muravyev) suggests retail traders often avoid paying the spread, so this case is probably pessimistic.
  • Most daily marks are model prices. Outside the registered stress months, positions are marked with Black-76 at the fund's own Cboe index level, which misses the smile. The stress months, where margin calls cluster, use real OPRA quotes.
  • One trade a month, held to expiry. Early exercise of these American options is ignored, and nothing is rolled or hedged.
  • Excluded months cluster in crashes. Months that failed the registered data checks are left out. Added back, they make gold's result worse (from −3.8 to −9.3 cents per dollar of premium at mid) but not oil's: USO earns +7.6 instead of +6.9, once the one trade held across its 2020 reverse split is settled on the adjusted contract.
  • The free-data error assumes a constant ratio. It was measured over three weeks in 2026 and applied to ten years.
  • Nothing here touches a registered result. The paper's hypotheses and verdicts are unchanged by this study.
