**Title:** What Does Free Options Data Cost? Measuring a Retail-Grade Feed Against Cboe's Volatility Indices

Students, retail investors, and small researchers often price options with free "indicative" quotes, because the consolidated market feed costs thousands of dollars a year. How much precision that free data gives up has not been measured. Since September 2026, I have recorded the free options feed of Alpaca, a retail brokerage, every trading day for 109 tickers. I compare it with three references: the volatility indices published by Cboe Global Markets (Cboe), consolidated quotes from the Options Price Reporting Authority (OPRA), and Bloomberg. Each hypothesis is committed with a fixed pass/fail bar before its data exists, and failures are reported. As validation, the method recovers the documented volatility risk premium on ten years of Cboe data (9 of 11 pairs). Preliminary results suggest the free quotes are not the main problem: their prices sit within a small fraction of the bid-ask spread of the consolidated market. Most of the gap comes from how quotes are turned into a volatility estimate. Using only strikes within 10% of the price reads oil volatility about 12 points below the Cboe Crude Oil Volatility Index (OVX); widening to 30% cuts the average gap to 0.6 points. One registered test has failed: skipping zero-bid quotes did not bring the estimate within 0.5 points of OVX. Final readings are due in mid-November. The work matters because if the error lies in method rather than data, anyone without a data budget can remove most of it for free, and these results show where to start.
