## The question (PROTOCOL step 1)
H1 was registered on 6 Sep for 1990–2026 and fell back to 2016–2026, because the free price feed only

Compustat through WRDS (Marc Vinyard) - S&P 500 from 1990, DJIA from 1997, Nasdaq-100 from 2001, Russell

## Hypothesis (proposed wording)
**H10a.** For each of VIX/SPX, VXD/DJIA, VXN/NDX and RVX/RUT, the mean of `IV(t) − RV(t, t+21)` over
non-overlapping 21-trading-day windows that **end before 4 Jan 2016** (H1's first day, so no window is
shared with H1) is positive, with a two-sided fixed-b Newey–West p-value under 0.05 (inference below).
Headline: how many of the four, with Benjamini–Hochberg and Benjamini–Yekutieli beside it, as H1.

Descriptive, no prediction, printed beside and never folded into H10a:
- **D1. The whole sample**, from each start to the data end (2 Nov 2026), per pair.
- **D2. H1's proxy caveat, measured on H1's own windows.** H1's observation windows for its four equity
  pairs are regenerated by H1's own code and inputs (`build_sample_a.py`, its Cboe IV and fund closes,
  `--end 2026-09-06` as the 9 Oct reproduction). For each window, the realized volatility is recomputed on
  the index closes at the **same two endpoint dates**. With the IV identical, the premium difference is
  exactly `RV_fund − RV_index`: its mean and mean absolute value per pair, and the count of windows whose
  endpoints have no index close (unmatched, listed). H1's verdict stands as recorded whatever D2 shows. A
  fresh grid on the index calendar is not D2; if ever wanted, it is separate and descriptive.
- **D3. Grid phase.** All 21 offsets of the non-overlapping grid: the range of the mean premium and of the
  test statistic per pair. The headline uses offset 0, anchored at the first common date, as H1 did.
- **D4. A range-based realized volatility (Parkinson 1980).** *Specified 5 Nov 2026, after Marc's highs and
  lows arrived and before any of their values was read. Only the files' structure had been read: the
  header, row counts, and first and last dates.*
  - **Windows:** H10a's and D1's own windows, on the same 21 trading days t+1 ... t+21 whose returns H1's RV
    uses.
  - **Formula:** `RV_P(t, t+21) = sqrt(252 x mean over those days of (ln(H/L))^2 / (4 ln 2))`, where H and L
    are the day's high and low. There is no c4 factor, since c4 corrects the close-to-close estimator only.
  - **Per pair:** the mean of `IV(t) - RV_P(t, t+21)`, with the registered fixed-b Newey-West statistic
    printed beside it (no verdict), and the mean of `RV_P - RV` over the same windows.
  - **Known biases, all pushing RV_P down:** the range misses the overnight move, which close-to-close
    returns include; a high and low sampled at discrete moments understate the true range; and the square
    root of an unbiased variance is biased low. A larger premium under D4 is therefore expected by
    construction and is no evidence about H10a.
  - **Usable days:** a day is usable only if its high and low are present and positive, its high is above
    its low, and low <= close <= high. A window enters D4 only if all 21 of its days are usable. Other
    windows are counted and listed by year, never filled.
  - **Definition changes:** if a source's documentation shows its daily high and low changed definition
    inside the sample (for example, from extremes computed from each member's own high and low to the
    index's actual intraday extremes), the date is a method break for D4, handled as the Specification
    handles the closes' breaks. This is read from the documentation, never inferred from the values.
  - **Sources, fixed now:** for the S&P 500, DJIA and Nasdaq-100, Compustat's `prchd` and `prcld`, checked
    against Compustat's close (`prccd`, the close H10 uses). For the Russell 2000, which Compustat lacks,
    Bloomberg's `PX_HIGH` and `PX_LOW` from the 23-25 Nov pull, checked against Bloomberg's own close
    (`PX_LAST`). If that pull brings no usable Russell 2000 highs and lows, D4 reports the pair as not
    measured.
  - **Cross-check:** Bloomberg's highs and lows for the other three, as the distribution of daily
    differences in ln(H/L) between the two sources on common days, beside decision 4's comparison of
    their closes.
  - Nothing in D4 changes after its values are seen. It is printed beside H10a and never folded into it.

## Specification (proposed; frozen at registration)
- `IV(t)` = the Cboe index close on date t, divided by 100. **Only FRED's reprints reach the early years
  for three of the four:** Cboe's own history files start 1990-01-02 for VIX but only in mid-September 2009
  for VXD, VXN and RVX (`samples/long/SOURCES.md`, checked 6 Sep: "FRED reaches further"), while FRED's
  VIXCLS starts 1990-01-02, VXDCLS 1997-10-07, VXNCLS 2001-02-02 and RVXCLS 2004-01-02. Proposed: FRED for
  all four, one source throughout, credited "Cboe, via FRED", with Cboe's own files compared on their
  overlap (noise floor below). The source is fixed in this file before the run: the two can carry
  different dates (FRED had four EVZ dates Cboe's file lacked, which shifted H1's grid in the 9 Oct
  reproduction).
- Prices: Compustat Index Prices – Daily, close (`prccd`), gvkeyx 000003 (S&P 500), 000005 (DJIA), 000208
  (Nasdaq-100), 156765 (Russell 2000), as listed in the files' own key column. Price indices: the series
  the index options are written on.
- `RV(t, t+21)` = `analyze.realized_vol(analyze.log_returns(...))`: un-demeaned, c4-corrected, imported
  rather than reimplemented, exactly as H1.
- Grid: `samples/long/build_sample_a.observations`, imported unchanged: a 21-trading-day step; a start
  date with no IV, or whose window spans more than 45 calendar days, moved on by one day. Trading days are
  the Compustat close dates. Start: the first date with both series (1990-01-02, 1997-10-07, 2001-02-02,
  2004-01-02). A window belongs to H10a only if its last date is before 2016-01-04.
- **Method breaks** (see Instrument readiness): if a pair's index history changes calculation method
  inside the sample, the series is split at the documented date into segments, each passed to the grid on
  its own: no window crosses the date, and the grid starts afresh on the segment's first date (as H1's
  grid does after a delisting gap, because the 45-day rule cannot see a method change while prices run on).
  H10a reads each segment and the pair's pooled windows, both printed. Exchange closures are not method
  breaks. If the notes show one method throughout, no segmentation is applied and that is recorded.
  PROTOCOL step 5 already lists two breaks in the long Cboe sample, the index filter (8 Jul 2024) and the
  zero-bid-or-zero-ask rule (10 Feb 2025, VIX included): both fall after H10a's cut-off but inside D1.
- **Inference (proposed; Gabriel decides, item 2).** PROTOCOL step 4 makes Newey–West with fixed-b
  critical values the house method, so it carries the verdict: the t-statistic from
  `analyze.newey_west(premia, L)`, with `L` frozen now as the whole number nearest 1.3√N (N = the pair's
  window count; the rule Lazarus, Lewis, Stock and Watson 2018 recommend for Newey–West with fixed-b
  critical values, and the one H8's draft names), so about 23, 19, 17 and 16 at the planning counts below;
  the p-value from `analyze.fixed_b_pvalue(t, N, L)` (its fixed seed and 4,000 simulated draws). H1's own
  test, `analyze.plain_t`, is printed beside it for comparability with H1. If the two disagree, both are
  printed and the disagreement is stated; the verdict is the registered test's, fixed here, so nothing is
  left to choose after seeing which passes (SCIENTIST.md). `analyze.sign_test` is printed beside, and BH
  and BY are applied to the four fixed-b p-values.

## Noise floor [required]
H10a tests the sign and significance of a mean, not agreement with a reference, so its floor is sampling
error (next section). The data sources are measured before registration, not assumed adequate:
- **Prices:** Compustat against a second source where one exists, on their overlap: FRED's NASDAQ100
  (from 1986, so all of NDX's sample), SP500 and DJIA (about the last ten years only). Reported as the
  distribution of daily log-return differences and, through the frozen estimator, the resulting RV

## Smallest detectable effect [required]
A **conditional planning approximation** (PROTOCOL step 4), not a power analysis of the registered test.
Windows are counted on the Compustat dates (dates only) by stepping 21 from FRED's IV start; the
registered N will differ slightly (dates with no IV, dropped spans, any segmentation). σ is H1's per-window
spread on the matching funds, from its recorded table: σ = mean ÷ t × √N. **The counts hold only on FRED's
longer series:** on Cboe's own files VXD, VXN and RVX would start in September 2009 and leave about 75
windows each before 2016 (counted on Cboe's cached dates), detectable 2.33 to 2.67 even when independent.

| pair | H1 pair: N, mean, t | σ | windows ending before 2016 | detectable, independent | σ ×1.5 | σ ×1.5, ρ = 0.4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| VIX/SPX | VIX/SPY: 127, +3.52, 5.14 | 7.72 | 312 | 1.22 | 1.84 | 2.80 |
| VXD/DJIA | VXD/DIA: 127, +3.26, 5.02 | 7.32 | 218 | 1.39 | 2.08 | 3.18 |
| VXN/NDX | VXN/QQQ: 127, +2.70, 3.68 | 8.27 | 178 | 1.74 | 2.60 | 3.98 |
| RVX/RUT | RVX/IWM: 127, +3.07, 4.80 | 7.21 | 143 | 1.69 | 2.53 | 3.87 |

Points of annualised volatility; detectable ≈ 2.8σ/√N (PROTOCOL's normal approximation, 2.8 = 1.96 +
0.84), per pair, before any multiplicity adjustment (BH and BY over four make it slightly larger).
Non-overlapping windows are not independent: premia cluster in turbulent years. The last column scales by
√((1+ρ)/(1−ρ)) for a first-order autocorrelation of 0.4 (ρ = 0.2 gives ×1.22); ρ is not known for these
years and is not estimated from them before the test. Under the proposed fixed-b test the 5% critical
values at the planning lags are 2.16 to 2.26, not 1.96 (simulated with `analyze.fixed_b_null`, 4 Nov),
which raises every figure by 7–11%.
**Reading,** against each pair's own H1 premium (3.52, 3.26, 2.70, 3.07): VIX/SPX is detectable in every
case (3.04 at worst, with fixed-b). VXD/DJIA is detectable except in the harshest case with fixed-b (3.40
against 3.26). VXN/NDX and RVX/RUT are not detectable in the harshest case either way, and VXN/NDX is on
the edge at σ ×1.5 (2.60, or 2.83 with fixed-b, against 2.70). A miss on any pair but VIX/SPX would not,
by itself, point at a fault.

## What would falsify it
H10a fails for a pair if its mean premium is not positive with the registered p-value under 0.05. The
premium is documented for these indices and years (Carr & Wu 2009, already cited here; the rest of the
prior art is to be read and cited before registering, SCIENTIST step 3). So a failure on VIX/SPX points
first at the data or the loader; a failure on the other three is read with the power figures above. Every
failure is reported as a failure.
